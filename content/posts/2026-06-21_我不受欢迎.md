**Preface**

The author is an ordinary person. On intellect alone, he could never pass those interview questions designed to select for genius. But he has watched AI's advance with his own eyes and felt dread.

You could say he is someone standing on the horizon, watching a rocket launch, raging at his own limits.

In that sense, what follows may be read as the yapping of an incompetent, the hypocrisy of a self-appointed moral sentry, the anti-intellectualism of the weak.



**1.0**

On May 18, the New York Times reported that Anthropic employees felt they were being "targeted" in a series of recent events[1]. The immediate cause: the US government had ordered the company to cut off all non-citizens from Fable 5 — including Anthropic's own foreign employees — and Anthropic responded by pulling the model entirely[2]. In an unprecedented move, Anthropic even stood up for a competitor: the very capabilities that had triggered US restrictions on Fable 5 already existed in OpenAI's GPT-5.5[2].

But GPT-5.5 was still alive and well.

How could they not feel wronged? The competitor that never took safety seriously still had its product intact, while Anthropic had made safety its foundational value all along.

In the name of safety, they nobly restricted Fable 5's performance in certain frontier domains — large-model development, biochemistry research — and, without informing users, silently swapped the responding model for Opus 4.8[3].

In the name of safety, they selectively opened Mythos — a model deemed "too difficult to control" — to a few hundred large institutions for "safety research." But what those institutions actually did with it had nothing to do with cybersecurity.

In the name of safety, they antagonized the Pentagon[4], openly staged the opposition between "democratic AI" and "authoritarian AI"[5], wrote their usage policy as a moral map, and banned accounts for uses they deemed out of bounds — even when the splash damage hit many bystanders.

I think this is a kind of candor. Compared to OpenAI and its endless talk of the masses, of universal access, of consensus, Anthropic has never shied from baring its unapologetic elitism. Its monopoly on defining "safety" is just one example.

What baffles me is how staggeringly unprepared they are for the blowback these choices inevitably invite.

What I mean is: you tell everyone in the room, day after day, that they are garbage. Not being targeted would be the surprise. So why the victim act?

I have no doubt that Anthropic was founded because OpenAI had lost its way. But at this point in the story, the dragon-slayer-turned-dragon is a tragedy only when the dragon knows it has become the villain. If the dragon still thinks itself innocent, the whole fable degrades into the laughable legend of a man-child.



**2.0**

Anthropic, to my eyes, lacks a fundamental civic common sense and empathy. Behind many of its policies hides a naïve despotism: We are the elite, therefore we have made the right choice. The masses — or the rabble — need only obey.

Those who have never been touched by the muck of society will fundamentally misjudge the complexity of its currents. And Anthropic is full of exactly such people: people who believe social progress is driven by a handful of elites, while the public plays only the roles of victim, obstacle, and riffraff. Therefore, to fully realize the ideal, a paternalism must be imposed from the top down: a Moses to lead the way and hand down the Ten Commandments, for a public that cannot yet see the future.

If you happen to have an East Asian parent — a tiger mom — you will not have been spared this line during your rebellious teenage years: *But I'm doing all this for your own good! Even if you won't show gratitude, at least don't be so ungrateful.*

I think Anthropic carries this mentality much of the time.



**3.0**

The other day, Anthropic's CEO said in an interview that he has only one direct report[6]. Reports also say he regularly posts essay-length pieces on the company Slack, laying out strategy, culture, and decision-making[7]. Clearly, this is someone with his reasoning effort set to xhigh — a person who likes to think deep. How much of that test-time compute is overfitting, though, is anyone's guess.

You could say he belongs to AI's "thinker" school. Silicon Valley has another, more pragmatic school: the "doers." Reports say Jensen Huang was still a Washington outsider when the first chip export ban dropped, but afterward he began working there on a semi-permanent basis, moved GTC's stage to the political center, and delivered markedly MAGA-flavored remarks at every suitable occasion[8]. The efforts paid off: before the US–China summit, Air Force One made a special stop in Alaska just for him[9], and the original blanket chip ban did gain more room for maneuvering[10].

In college I read *The Temple of the Golden Pavilion* and then racked my brains over what Mishima meant by the distinction between "knowledge oriented toward action" and "action oriented toward knowledge." I did not truly understand it until years later. My current answer is the line from the Analects: "To learn without thinking is to be lost; to think without learning is perilous" — where *learning* means not just study in the narrow sense, but acquiring out-of-distribution samples.

Anthropic's CEO is plainly deficient in this *think-and-learn*. He has sealed himself inside an echo chamber at the tip of an ivory tower and calls it "focus" and "filtering out noise."

I find one thing especially telling. When setting out their visions for social and political governance, OpenAI's narrative speaks of universal basic income and tax redistribution[11]; Anthropic writes: we must build an IAEA-like coalition with the power to make everyone "pause AI research"[12].

Can you see the difference between "knowledge oriented toward action" and "action oriented toward knowledge" in these two manifestos?



**4.0**

Ignorance of the political landscape is why Anthropic draws hostility, but woven into that hostility is my admiration for its stubbornness — because it is their product, first and foremost, that earns them the right to posture. What I want to avoid becoming is what Akutagawa Ryūnosuke called "mediocrities who cannot produce even a scandal of their own, yet find in every eminent person's scandal the best weapon to defend their cowardice and incompetence — and the pedestal on which to erect a superiority that does not actually exist."[13]

I hope they stay exactly this way. Because from a business-strategy standpoint, their stubbornness forces every other AI company toward greater accessibility, eventually giving ordinary people like us a little more room to breathe. As Altman said in response to Anthropic's mocking Super Bowl ad this year: unlike a competitor that serves the rich, we need ads to survive, because we need to provide AI to people who can't afford a subscription[14].

If, by some misfortune, something makes Anthropic slicker — smooth enough to slide into direct competition for OpenAI's ecological niche — God knows what moral backsliding the ever-cunning Altman could justify under the banner of "business necessity."

So, Anthropic: please, keep being unpopular!

**序**

作者是一个普通的人，他凭智力根本不可能通过那些专为选拔天才而设的面试题。但他也确实对AI的进步看在眼里并感到惶恐。

因此你可以说他是个站在地平线上看着火箭升空而对自己的界限感到愤怒的人。

从这个意义上，以下文字可以看作是一个无能者的狺狺，一个道德标兵的伪善，一个弱势群体的反智之言。



**1.0**

5月18号，纽约时报的专栏中报道道：Anthropic的员工在近期的一系列事件中感到被“针对”了[1]。这个反应的直接原因，是美国政府要求该公司切断除美国公民外的人员对Fable 5的使用（这包括Anthropic自己的外籍员工），然后Anthropic就整个下架了这个模型[2]。这个事件中Anthropic也是破天荒为友商站了一次台：导致美国限制的Fable 5的能力，其实在OpenAI 的 GPT-5.5 中已经具备了[2]。

但GPT-5.5还是活得好好的。

这怎么能不委屈呢？那个根本不把安全当回事儿的友商的产品依然健在，而Anthropic可是从头至尾都把安全作为根本价值观的啊。

为了安全，他们大义凛然限制Fable 5在一些前沿领域（比如大模型开发、生物化学研究）的发挥，在不告知用户的情况下，把应答模型替换为Opus 4.8[3]。

为了安全，他们选择性地把Mythos这个“太难以控制”的模型开放给几百个大型机构做“安全研究”。但这些机构拿着它做的却是和网络安全无关的事情。

为了安全，他们惹恼五角大楼[4]，公开鼓吹“民主AI”和“威权AI”的对立[5]，把使用政策写成一张道德地图，对他们认为越界的用途直接禁封账号，哪怕这些做法会溅射攻击很多人。

我觉得这是一种直率。比起OpenAI天天强调什么大众什么普惠什么共识，Anthropic从来都不惮于袒露自己独断的精英主义。对安全的定义权只是其中之一。

让我感到不解的只是他们对这种选择会招致的反馈令人震惊地毫无准备。

我的意思是，你们天天都在说在座的各位都是垃圾，不被针对才比较意外吧。为什么反而做出一副受害者的姿态呢？

我一点不怀疑Anthropic成立的初衷是因为OpenAI变味了。但故事写到这里，屠龙者终变恶龙是个悲剧的前提是恶龙对自己的反派身份有所自觉。如果恶龙仍觉得自己无辜的话，整个寓言就会降格为令人哑然失笑的巨婴传说。



**2.0**

Anthropic在我看起来缺乏一种根本性的公民常识和共情。他们出产的很多政策后面都隐藏着一个天真的独裁：我们是精英，所以我们做出了正确的选择。大众（或愚民）只需要听话就可以了。

从来没有被社会的污泥沾染过的人会对洋流的复杂性有根本性的误判。可Anthropic里面偏偏全是这样的人：认为社会进步只会由一小撮人推动，而大众在其中充当受害者、阻碍者和氓流的角色。因此，为了彻底实现理想，必须自上而下地施行一种家长主义：在公众看不清楚未来的时候，成为引路和制定十诫的摩西。

如果你碰巧有一个东亚家长/tiger mom，你叛逆的青春期中不会少听到这么一句话：——可是我都是为你好啊！你就算不报恩，至少也不能这么不领情吧。

我觉得Anthropic在很多时候都怀揣着这种心态。



**3.0**

前些日子Anthropic的CEO接受采访时说，自己只有一个direct report[6]。又有报道说他经常在公司的slack里面发些阐述战略、文化和决策之类的长文[7]。显然，这是一个reasoning effort设定在xhigh的喜欢深度思考的人。但他的这些test-time compute里有多少过拟合就不得而知了。

你可以说他属于AI的“思考派”。硅谷里还有另一种更务实的“行动派”。有报道称，黄仁勋在最初的芯片禁运令的时候还是个华盛顿的门外汉，但之后他就开始在这里半驻场地工作，把GTC的舞台搬到政治中心，而且每逢适合的场合就讲些非常MAGA的话[8]。这些做法收到了效果：中美峰会前空军一号还专门在阿拉斯加为他停了一站[9]，而原本一刀切的芯片禁令也确实有了更大的斡旋空间[10]。

我在大学时读了《金阁寺》，然后就绞尽脑汁思考过小说中探讨的“面向行动的认识”和“面向认识的行动”的区别到底是什么的问题，但真正理解还要到几年之后。我目前的对此的回答是“学而不思则罔，思而不学则殆”——学不仅是狭义的学习，而是获得out-of-distribution samples。

Anthropic的CEO显然很缺乏这个“思而学”。他把自己封闭在一个象牙塔尖的回音壁里，并美其名曰“专注”和“屏蔽噪音”。

我觉得很有代表性的一件事情是，在阐述公司的社会/政治治理方针时，OpenAI的叙事里讲到了全民基本收入、税收再分配[11]；但Anthropic写道：我们要建立一个像国际原子能机构一样的联盟可以有权力让所有人“暂停AI研究”[12]。

你能理解这两份宣言性政策中“面向行动的认识”和“面向认识的行动”的区别吗？



**4.0**

对政治生态的无知是Anthropic招恨的原因，但这种恨意里有我对他们固执的佩服。因为首先是他们的产品让他们有摆谱的资格。我想避免的是芥川龙之介所谓“连丑闻都制造不出的凡夫俗子们在所有名士的丑闻中找出了足以辩护自己怯懦无能的最好武器，同时物色到了赖以树立自己实际上并不存在的优势的台基。”[13]

我期待他们一直这样下去。因为它会从商业策略的角度逼着其他AI公司朝着更加普惠的方向发展，最终让我们这些平民有机会多喘气一会儿。就像Altman在面对今年Anthropic超级碗的嘲讽广告时说：和服务富人的竞争对手不一样，我们需要广告来维生，因为我们需要给那些付不起订阅的人也提供AI[14]。

如果不幸有什么事情让Anthropic变得更加圆滑，终于滑到OpenAI的生态位直接竞争后，天知道老奸巨猾的Altman又能以“商业的必要性”为借口做出怎样的道德滑坡。

所以Anthropic，请继续不受欢迎下去吧！
