A six-foot deep-sea fishing champion, member of the Lost Generation, soldier, psychiatric patient, boxing enthusiast, and *cubano sato*—an “ordinary Cuban”—left Sloppy Joe’s late one night. He cast a bleary eye toward the lighthouse’s great column of light, then ambled home with a long shadow and a men’s-room urinal in tow. Home was the mansion with the largest private pool in town, a wedding gift from his second wife’s uncle. Snow White, the six-toed cat who lived there, knew nothing of this—and would not have cared if she had. Before long, the name of her housemate, Hemingway, would become the name people gave her entire clan.

I stood in the garden where Snow White’s descendants lived—or rather, sprawled—as towering tropical plants rustled in the onshore Atlantic wind. Sunlight sifted down like fine drizzle through a small clump of mottled bamboo. Upstairs in the main house, a ceiling fan turned lazily; even from a distance, it seemed to waft over that peculiar coolness of the tropics. Across the way, in the carriage house’s upstairs writing studio, a Royal typewriter sat on a modest round table. An antlered deer head was nailed to the wall; a military pack rested on the floor against the wall.

Every morning at six, he entered his study. At noon, he left. He spent his afternoons and evenings deep-sea fishing—or dutifully following the authoritative prescription for hereditary bipolar disorder: heavy drinking. Hangovers may have been the norm. Even so, over some nine years, this typewriter kept hammering out words that became *A Farewell to Arms*, *Men Without Women*, *Green Hills of Africa*, “The Snows of Kilimanjaro” … If I had once been enthralled by the “beautiful-form” trajectory of Yukio Mishima’s life—his precipitous transformation from a slight young man—Hemingway’s life was almost too **American**.

He seemed to have lived through everything: champion deep-sea fisherman, member of the Lost Generation, soldier, psychiatric patient, boxing enthusiast, “ordinary Cuban.” More important, as a well-built, hard-edged man, he embodied a philosophy forged through “action”—through the peculiarly American sense of mission behind his romantic restlessness, and through the deep-seated egocentrism that drove him to carry out his own vision of justice. It was a way of thinking diametrically opposed to Kantian contemplation.

To my mind, he was a poor representative of the Lost Generation. The reason is simple: he was never lost in the first place. Very early on, he had already found in heroism a definite form of self-expression. The truly lost one was his friend, the poet and dreamer F. Scott Fitzgerald, whose talent sprang chiefly from a heaven-sent sensitivity and melancholy. Where Fitz forever reached for dreams beyond his grasp, Hemingway drew his talent from a worship of the **ideal**. Fitz’s writing was sorrow leaking out; Hemingway’s was force spilling over.

It had never occurred to me that a writer might exist as Hemingway did. The writers I admired—W. Somerset Maugham, Marcel Proust, Yukio Mishima …—all made literature out of a “rejection of life.” Hemingway, that intensely American writer, made his through a “traversal of life.” Put another way, he belonged to a rare breed of writer: one who conducts his search through “action,” not “imagination.”

I do not so much envy that way of living as recognize how fundamentally at odds it is with my own temperament. But I do, after all, live in his native land. Perhaps the pragmatism in this country’s bones will remake me beyond recognition. Perhaps even that is something I would welcome with open arms.

一名身高六英尺的海钓冠军、迷惘的一代、军人、精神病患、拳击爱好人士、“普通的古巴人”离开了深夜的邋遢乔（Sloppy Joe）酒吧，他迷离望向灯塔巨大的柱形光亮，然后拖着长长的影子和一个男厕，踱回坐拥镇上最大私人泳池的洋房——他第二任妻子的叔叔送给他们的结婚礼物。家里的六趾猫咪白雪并不知道（就算知道也不在乎），不久之后，这个同居者的名字，海明威，将成为了人们给予它们这一族的称谓。

我站在白雪后代居留（四处躺倒）的庭园里，高耸的热带绿植在大西洋的向岸风中簌簌响着。而阳光从一小撮斑竹的叶条中被淅沥沥地筛下来，主楼二层的吊扇懒洋洋地转动，远远地带来一种热带地区独有的清凉感。对面的马车库里，二楼书室中央一张不大的圆桌上摆放着皇家打字机，墙上钉着长着犄角的鹿头标本，靠墙处的地板上则摆放着一个军旅背包。

他每日六时走进自己的书房，中午时分离开；下午和晚上则出海钓鱼、或遵照医嘱执行针对家族遗传型躁郁症的权威处方：酗酒。宿醉也许会成为常态，但无论如何，其中的某九年间，这个打字机上曾经连续拼下了这样一些单词：永别了、武器；没有女人的男人；非洲的青山；乞力马扎罗的雪……如果说以前的我曾醉心于三岛由纪夫纤细少年断崖式成长的“美形”式的生活轨迹，那么海明威的生命则简直太过**“美式”**。

他似乎全部都经历了——作为海钓冠军、迷惘的一代、军人、精神病患、拳击爱好人士、“普通的古巴人”，但或许最重要的，作为一个健美而强硬的男人，他以这个国家特有的浪漫主义式躁动的使命感，和贯彻自身正义的根深蒂固的自我中心，展现出一种被“行动”所塑造的哲学——和康德式沉思截然相反的思考模式。

在我的观念中，他是“迷惘的一代”不合格的代表。原因很简单，他根本就不迷茫，他很早就找到了英雄确定形式的表达。真正迷惘的，是他的朋友——具有梦想家气质的诗人——菲兹杰拉德，这种才华主要起源于天赐的敏感和忧郁。和菲兹对梦想不可及的憧憬不同，海明威的才华主要来源于对**理想**的崇拜；可以说，菲兹的文字是哀伤感的泄露，而海明威的文字是力量感的溢出。

应该说，我从未意识到作家可以以海明威的方式存在的可能性。我所钦慕的人们——毛姆、普鲁斯特、三岛由纪夫…，他们的文字无不成就于“对生活的拒绝”；而作为一个非常美式的作家， 海明威的文字则成就于“对生活的遍历”。换种方式说，海明威是稀有派的，以“行动”而非“想象力”来实践自身追寻的人。

但与其说我羡慕这种生存方式，不如说我深知这种生存方式和我自身气质有根本性的抵触。但毕竟我生活在他的故土呢，也许这个国家骨子里的实践主义会把我改造得面目全非也说不定。我对此也许也是张开双臂欢迎的。
