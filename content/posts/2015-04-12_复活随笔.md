【Rome · Vatican City】

There were once people who spent their entire lives doing nothing but trying to render the face of Jesus. Watching camera flashes beat relentlessly against the statues, I thought that perhaps they alone deserved to be called truly ambitious.

The whole church was one vast altar, its elaborate ornament the crystallized wealth of an entire generation—and perhaps, from beginning to end, the whole thing had existed only to serve the desires of a few. Yet its value cannot be denied. Even if only for the sake of those who come to worship, its value cannot be denied. As someone for whom religion alone is not enough, I saw in it the radiance of the human spirit, aesthetically and architecturally. I imagined that every artisan working on the scaffolds, bending beneath the dome to look down at the town spread across the ground, must have had one fleeting moment when they half believed they were building the Tower of Babel. This is a great undertaking, as it has been in every age.

【Florence】

Wandering through the Uffizi Galleries, I saw many Jesuses and many Marys. The Jesuses and Marys wore all kinds of faces. That much was obvious: from the Middle Ages through the early Renaissance—the periods to which those painters belonged—religious subjects predominated. But it made me wonder: if every painter carried within them a different Platonic Form for Jesus and for Mary, could it be that neither was a single individual at all, but a set? Yes. In the great current of history, anyone who leads by example and carries humanity’s fate forward may be given the name Jesus; every chaste and noble person is also part of the Virgin. The definitions of Jesus and the Virgin are continually enriched, and that is precisely what allows them to appear with so many different faces. They stand for what humanity pursues, expects, and desires.

As I climbed the winding, cramped, dark passageways of the Cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore, I had the strange sense that I was making my way upstream through the cathedral’s esophagus. The figures ahead tilted and swayed until, at moments, I mistook them for afterimages left by visitors who had reached the top long before me. And in truth, why not? The cathedral ingests and disgorges thousands of people every day; who could possibly be remembered by this thing that has stood for centuries?

From the viewing platform atop the dome, I gazed down almost imperiously over Florence, over its red-and-white houses caught between hills and white clouds. I imagined that this behemoth, from the moment its construction was complete, had somehow begun a new life of its own. It no longer belonged to humanity. It had become something like a spirit medium: a solid fortress reaching flamboyantly into the sky. On the cathedral’s scale of time, the tourists and residents who have crowded this city for centuries are no more than flowing water—if all eras are superimposed upon one another. Under the sun, that water keeps evaporating from the channels divided by the red-and-white houses. Seen from the distant hills, the houses themselves are nothing but the cathedral’s vast root system, spreading in every direction so that it may grow.

Only the cathedral remains. The cathedral alone is truly alive.

Humanity! How pale and powerless you look before this thing of your own making! And yet perhaps that is precisely why we had to make the cathedral.

Let us stay with the metaphor of flowing water. Once the shell of the body slips free of the shackles of its own consciousness, we too can see that the images of an entire life are nothing but water that has washed through it. Yes. As Haruki Murakami wrote, “I was nothing but a pathway for the person known as me.”

【Cinque Terre】

Motionless on time’s leeward slope

【Train Reverie】

Haruki Murakami’s creative process tells us simply this about the search through life: we seek hope and meaning, only to watch them recede into the intangible. Then we affirm the worth of our own striving and endurance. We affirm our knowledge that, having fallen into the human world, there is something we must bear. At last we begin to believe in the power of love. We become warriors of love and set out along a road governed entirely by causes and conditions. Those who have truly set foot on that road have perhaps come to know that love is the core of the human spirit. With that certainty, they wait in happiness—or, within happiness, they grow. Yes. Love makes the world go round.

【罗马·梵蒂冈】

从前有些人，光是为了临摹耶稣的容貌就花上了一辈子。我看着那些被闪光灯不停拍打的雕像，心想也许只有他们，才配被称为真正的野心家。

整个教堂就是一个大祭坛，那些繁复的装饰凝结了一整个世代的财富——而也许从头至尾只是为了满足某些人的欲望。可是它的价值无法被否定，即使只是为了那些朝拜的人它的价值也不能被否定。作为一个不单纯满足宗教的人，我从中看到的是人类精神熠熠的闪光，从美学的意义上，从建筑学的意义上。我想每一个在梯架上工作的工匠，当他们在穹顶俯身看向铺在地上的市镇的时候，他们总有一个瞬间恍惚相信自己正在修建巴别塔。这是一项伟大的事业，从古至今都是。


【佛罗伦萨】

当我漫游在乌菲兹美术馆里的时候，我看到了许多的耶稣和许多的玛丽。耶稣和玛丽们拥有各式的面孔——这是显而易见的事——因为从画家的从属的时代来说，从中世纪到文艺复兴早期的作品大多是宗教题材。可是我就想，既然每个画家心中都拥有不同理型的耶稣和玛利亚的形象，有没有可能他们本身就不是一个个体，而是一个集合呢？是的，在这条历史的洪流中，所有以身作则，背负人类命运前进的人都可以被冠以耶稣的称号；所有贞洁而高尚的人也都是圣母的组成部分。耶稣和圣母的定义是逐渐在丰富的，而因此他们才得以显现不同的面孔。他们代表了人类的追求、期待和愿望。

攀爬百花圣母大教堂的弯曲、促狭而昏暗的甬道，我恍惚觉得自己是在教堂的食道里逆流而上。我前面的人影歪歪斜斜地摇曳着，以至于有些时刻我竟把它们当做以前登顶的游客留下的鬼影。其实又何尝不是呢？这个大教堂每天吞吐上千的游客，谁又有可能真正被这个矗立了几百年的东西记住呢？我站在穹顶的观景台上，睥睨翡冷翠夹在丘陵和白云之间红红白白的住宅。我想象着，这个庞然大物，自修建竣工之日起，就奇怪地自顾自地开始了新的生命，不再属于人类，而成为了类似灵媒的招展地伸向天空的坚固堡垒。在它的时间刻度里，这个城市几百年来熙熙攘攘的游客和居民不过构成了一种水流般的东西——如果我们将所有的时代重叠起来的话。这些流水在太阳底下不断蒸发着，从那些被红红白白的房子隔开的沟洫里；而从远处的山峦眺望过来，这些住宅也不过是教堂为了生长而延展四方的庞大根系！

剩下来的唯独教堂，真正活着的只有教堂。

人类！你们在面对这个自身创造之物时显得多么苍白无力！可是也许正是因为这样，我们才有必要把教堂创造出来吧。

让我们继续水流的隐喻。当我们的躯壳也脱离了自身意识的枷锁，我们同样可以看见自己一生的影像不过是这副躯壳中冲刷而过的水流。是的，正如村上春树先生所说，“我不过是我这个人的一条通道”。

【火车狂想】

村上春树先生的创作过程，无非告诉我们这样一个探寻人生的事实：我们追寻希望和意义，却往往遁于缥缈；而后我们肯定自身奋斗和忍耐的价值，肯定自身因堕入人世而必须背负什么的认识；最后我们开始相信爱的力量，化身爱的战士，踏上一条全然被因缘所左右的道路。真正踏上这条道路的人大概知晓了爱才是人类精神的内核。他们怀着这种确信，幸福中等待，或者、在幸福中生长。是的，世界因爱而转动。
