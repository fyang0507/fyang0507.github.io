BOARD 02 · ROUND 2

The pen, in three tiers 笔：三级强调

Round 1 gave every state its own mark, and Fred liked three of them. Used side by side, an underline, a wash and an arrow are three vocabularies on one page. Used as tiers they become one sentence said louder: notice → choose → point, each mark growing out of the one before it by the same hand, and the single coral mark kept for the only thing a view asks of you.

RULE 1

Three tiers, one hand 三级，一只手

An element shows its highest tier. Lower tiers are absorbed, not removed: the tier-1 line survives as the band’s darker bottom edge. At rest (tier 0) nothing carries a mark except prose links, which keep a 1px pencil hairline so they can be found.

RULE 2

Up and down the tiers 升与降

0 → 1
pointer arrives: the underline draws left → right.
1 → 2
click / Enter: the pen doesn’t lift. It turns the chisel and comes back right → left; behind the tip the line thickens upward into the band; at the left end it flicks the tick.
0 → 2
tap or keyboard: the whole gesture at once — line in 170 ms, return stroke, tick.
2 → 1
closed while still under the pointer: the band sinks back into its line; the tick lifts.
2 → 0
the choice moves elsewhere: band sinks → line → line lifts, while the new choice draws.
press
momentary targets (links, Load more, ← →): 1 → 2 on press, held at least until the gesture lands, then back to 1.
interrupt
every reversal starts from the current frame. A half-grown band sinks from half height; nothing snaps.
3
draws once when the view is first seen; its target still uses tiers 1–2; using it lifts the arrow for the rest of the session (sessionStorage; it returns in a new browser session).
RULE 3

Coral, touch, stillness 珊瑚色、触摸、静止

coral
Tier 3 owns it, alone. Tier 3 is already rationed to one element per view — the same budget as the accent, so the two rules become one. Tier 1 follows the pointer: coral there turns the accent into a cursor and spends it on everything you pass (and it doesn’t exist on touch). Tier 2 is plural at rest — current page, selected tag, open card — so coral there spends it three times a view: the pill-chip problem again. Tiers 1–2 answer you; tier 3 is the only mark that speaks first.
title
the subpage title’s coral character (造 / 写) stays: it is fixed page identity, not interaction. The arrow is transient — first view in a session, lifted once used — so a brief overlap of the two is accepted.
so
resting link underlines and footnote numbers go pencil; the Building lead pin goes ink; a view whose arrow has been used has no interactive coral at all.
touch
no tier 1 — nothing sticks after a finger lifts. A tap is tier 2 directly (the 0 → 2 gesture); momentary targets play it through, then sink.
keyboard
Tab shows 「 」 only. Enter / Space is the 0 → 2 gesture.
reduced
every mark appears in its end state, instantly: band and tick at once, the arrow complete with no taps; retracts are instant.
THE MORPH

Key poses 关键帧

travel log · 游记 — hover, click, then another tag is chosen
return stroke · 回笔 — recommended
drag to scrub 0 → 1 → 2 → 0 · or click the words
press harder · 压笔 — the plainer alternative
the band rises in place from the line and the tick simply follows. Correct, calmer, and forgettable: no travel, no sense of one hand finishing a gesture.
RECOMMENDATION

A · three tiers, one hand, with the return-stroke morph. It keeps what Fred liked in B, C and D and gives them a grammar: an underline is interest, a wash is a decision, an arrow is a request — and each is visibly the previous mark said louder, because the band grows out of the line and the line survives inside it.

Watch: tier 3 must stay rare or it becomes a sale sticker — one per view, never on navigation, and it retires after use. The band needs ~4px of air past each end and the tick ~20px before a word, so keep tag gaps ≥ 26px. The subpage title’s coral character stays as fixed identity; its brief overlap with a first-view arrow is accepted.
