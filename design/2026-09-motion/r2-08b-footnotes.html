BOARD 08b · ROUND 2

Footnotes with a body 有身体的脚注

Round 1 fixed legibility: margin notes stopped being set in handwriting, and each note got its full reference instead of a truncated “Flas…”. But the pointing itself was only a line fading in. Nothing with mass moved, so a dotted leader and a loop read as a highlight, not an event. On a phone the note rose as a generic bottom sheet. Round 2 keeps the readable type and gives the note a body that answers when you point at it: a slip on a thread, a folded strip, or a catalogue card.

WHAT CHANGED
FROM ROUND 1
The animation and pointing effect looks dull, WOW me.

Round 2 has three bolder mechanisms, and in each one the note itself physically answers, not only a line. A: the note is tugged across the margin on a thread. B: the sentence parts and a folded strip unfolds between the lines. C: one annotator’s gesture pulls a catalogue card out of a drawer. Each plays a first-time choreography once, then a quick repeat so that hovering a dozen refs stays fast. The phone note is now a slip pulled out of a slot cut in the page, and you swipe it back in. The note type is unchanged: upright serif, utility-mono dates, never handwriting for a citation.

ATugged slip 拽纸条coral thread under tension · slip on a spring, pivoting on its eyelet · physics clock
Each margin note is a paper slip with a punched eyelet. Point at a number and a coral thread knots around it, flies slack across the leading and catches the eyelet. At that instant it goes taut: it twangs once, the number gets tugged a hair, and the slip lurches out of the margin toward the sentence. The slip rises to the ref’s line and squares up to face the text, pivoting on the eyelet with its trailing edge lagging. Let go and the thread slackens and reels back into the knot, and the slip drifts home to its tilt. Hovering down the refs feels like plucking strings. first knot → throw → catch → lurch, reads by ~450 ms, settles by ~800 · repeat no throw, stiffer spring, ~300 ms · phone slip from the slot
cost one path and one transform per live note, and the rAF loop stops when everything is at rest · a11y refs stay real links, focus pulls the note just as hover does, Esc lets go, touch taps to pull · reduced motion the thread is simply drawn to the note where it rests, and nothing travels · accent the thread echoes the red string on Building’s corkboard
BUnfolds between the lines 行间展开text reflows around a strip of 3D-hinged flaps · each flap on its own spring · fold line on the hand’s clock
The margin goes quiet, and the notes live inside the text. Point at a number and a dashed coral fold line is drawn in the leading under its row, meaning “it opens here”. Click, Enter or tap parts the text exactly there. The visual line ends, a folded packet drops in, and its flaps unroll one after another from behind the tab. The gap is the paper’s projected height on every frame, so the paper itself pushes the lines apart; no tween does it. Click again, “fold ↑” or Esc folds it away and the lines close over it. Every crease falls between two lines of the note. first tab drops, flaps unroll in sequence, ~650–850 ms by length · repeat all flaps at once, ~300 ms · phone the same, because the column is the room
cost the gap’s height is the one layout property animated, bounded to about half a second; the hinges are transforms · a11y the ref is a disclosure (aria-expanded), the strip follows it in reading order, and its source links and fold button come next in tab order · loses glanceable margin notes, since a note is read only on request · reduced motion open / shut
CThe annotator’s hand 批注一笔 · 目录卡one continuous pen path, ⌟ → loop → arrow (hand’s clock) · catalogue card hinged at its foot (physics clock)
Pointing at a number plays one gesture, the way a reader with a pen marks a source. A corner ⌜ goes down where the claim starts. Then, without lifting, the pen draws a ⌟ where the claim ends, a loop around the number, and a swift arrow through the leading into the margin. As the arrow arrives, the note is pulled up out of its drawer as a library catalogue card: No. · host, who, title, date and the rod hole. The pen then underlines the title. The bracket is information, not decoration: it shows exactly which words the source backs. first touch down, lift, one stroke (580 ms), card at 455, underline by ~900 · repeat one quick stroke, card at 170, done by ~450 ms · phone slip from the slot
cost three paths and one card per reveal, WAAPI only, so nothing runs while idle · a11y refs stay links, focus plays the same gesture, the card is decorative and aria-hidden because the note is in the reference list · reduced motion the marks and card are simply there · risk the phrase finder is heuristic (a clause stop or ~34 characters back)
RECOMMENDATION

C, the annotator’s hand. It is the only one where the WOW also carries information. The ⌜⌟ shows exactly which words the source backs, the arrow shows where the source lives, and the card sets the citation out as fields: who, title, date, host. It is Fred’s thesis in one gesture, a person with a pen noticing a real source, and the catalogue card is an ordinary object given unusual attention. It stays fast: the first reveal takes about 0.9 s, and every one after is a single quick stroke of about 0.45 s. On a phone, the same card is pulled out of the slot and swiped back in.

A is the most visceral: the tug is a real physical surprise, and it rhymes with Building’s red string. But a dozen hovers means a dozen moving slips and a travelling coral line, which is more toy than reading aid. Keep it in reserve for a page with fewer notes. B is the boldest structurally and the best reading experience on a phone, because nothing covers the text. If Fred prefers text that parts to a slip that rises, use B’s unfold as C’s narrow-screen behaviour; both share one note model, so this is a swap, not a rebuild. Watch for three things. C’s phrase finder is a heuristic (a clause stop, or about 34 characters back), so an author-marked span would be exact. The card must stay aria-hidden, since the reference list is the accessible source. And the pen stays the view’s only coral.
