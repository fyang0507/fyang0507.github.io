FRED, ON ROUND 6

“The card got moved away from the original position and flipped, can we eliminate that unnecessary movement?”

The card stays where it is. A drag no longer carries it. It turns in place, a rotateY about its own centre, 10° as soon as it moves and up to 40° after a third of a card's width, toward the way you are dragging. Let go and the flip finishes from there, with one overshoot and no trip. The only other change while you hold it is a lift of a hair off the page.

Your pointer is what goes to the sleeve now, not the card. When the pointer enters the sleeve zone (the sleeve plus a margin past its mouth), the sleeve takes the card half in. That quick glide from its place to the mouth is the card's only travel, and the turn relaxes to 0. Move the pointer back out and the card glides home, and the turn picks up from your drag. Let go in the zone and it slides fully in; let go anywhere else and it flips in place. The sleeve shows it is the target before you get there: its lip bows toward your pointer in proportion to how near it is. The cue is ink; the coral 「 」 appears only once the pointer is in. On a phone the sleeve still steps aside to the left edge. The zone there is the strip plus the page margin beside it, 51 px wide and as tall as the card, so a thumb drag reaches it anywhere along the card.

RECOMMENDATION

Ship this as the About card. The card now behaves like a card you are holding. Turning it over doesn't move it, and it leaves your hand only when you put it back. The rule is still r6's single question, asked of your pointer: on the sleeve it goes back in; anywhere else it turns over. Both outcomes show before you let go: the card turning where it is, or the card half in the sleeve's mouth.

Watch: a drag that crosses the sleeve on its way somewhere else does take the card to the mouth and back, since the pointer passed through the zone. It returns to its exact place and then flips there. On a phone the zone is at the page edge, so a flip toward the left that runs all the way to the edge puts the card back instead; the half-inserted card shows that before you let go. Because the card no longer follows the finger, a drag carries no sense of weight. That is the trade Fred asked for.
