BOARD 04 · ROUND 2

The bookshelf, in depth 立体书架

In round 1, B's shelf was flat until you touched it. Hovering switched on perspective, tipped a spine and raised a separate bookmark card, so the depth arrived as a trick rather than a place. A had the right logic (you pull a book and open it), but its hover only lifted the spine, with no preview. Round 2 builds the shelf as paper boxes from the first frame. Pulling a book is then just the camera you were already looking through, and the preview is printed on the book itself.

WHAT CHANGED
FROM ROUND 1
  1. the 3D effect is too abrupt because the bookshelf is 2D to start3D from the first frame. One fixed camera sits above the plank with a shifted lens, so spines stay exactly their flat size. Every book is a paper box (page-block top, boards, fore-edge) on a plank with a front lip and a contact stipple. Nothing changes projection on hover.
  2. the added hover on top didn't really carry physical logicThe preview is on the book. Hover hooks the spine, slides the book out, lifts it clear and turns it in the hand. The real cover faces you and the facts are printed on a paper obi / 腰封 (A), or on the title page as the board cracks open (B). No slip, no card.
  3. A has a better logic of "opening the book", but it didn't provide the preview hoverClick continues the same gesture. From the held pose the book comes square, the board swings open to the ex libris and title page, and the title page grows into Reading. Back / Esc reverses it all and puts the book back in its slot.

WHAT CHANGES ON A PHONE

  • The shelf stays a 3D shelf. All 27 books sit on one plank as a native swipe strip. The camera is fixed to the screen, so as the shelf slides past, the far sides of the books change. You feel the depth with a thumb; no gyroscope needed.
  • Tap = pull. The first tap takes the book out and turns it to show its cover and band, and the strip recentres so it has room. Tap the book again, or its band, to open.
  • Tap anywhere else to put it back. The neighbours lean into the gap, then get shoved upright.
  • The bird comes along the tops, late, and sits on the book you are holding.

RECOMMENDATION

A: Pull it, read the band. It answers all three notes with one object. The shelf has depth before you touch it, so pulling a book is just the camera you were already looking through. The preview is printed on the thing you pulled, a real obi on its real cover. And the open is the same gesture, continued. The bird now has a reason to move: someone took a book down, and it wants to see.

Watch: the held book is big (a ≈190 px board) and briefly overlaps the heads of the row. The gap and the leaning neighbours stay visible under it, which is the point. B tells the stronger continuity story (the page you peek at is the page that arrives) but shows the cover at 40°; choose B if the reading page matters more than the cover. The camera numbers keep it Fred's shelf: the eye sits 660 px above the plank at a 4400 px perspective, which gives page-block tops of 11–15 px and side slivers only where a taller book stands next to a shorter one.
