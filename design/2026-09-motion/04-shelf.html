The bookshelf, physically 书架

The Writing shelf is a lovely idea rendered flat. Hovering a spine lifts it 20 px and swaps a detached, pure-white preview card on the far right, so the eye has to jump between spine and card; clicking hard-cuts to Reading. Spines have no depth, no weight, no neighbours, the yellow bird hops on a random timer and never cares what you are doing, and on phones the shelf is replaced by a generic swipe-card stack.

WHAT CHANGES ON A PHONE

  • The shelf stays a shelf. All 27 spines on one plank as a horizontal strip — native swipe, soft snap — instead of a swipe-card stack that hides everything but one card.
  • Tap = tip. The first tap tips the book and raises its slip; the strip recentres so the slip has room. A second tap on the book or on the slip opens it.
  • The slip keeps the cover. Nothing today's card shows is lost; it just rises out of the book you chose.
  • The bird walks the lip to the foot of the book you tapped — late, unhurried.

书架不再变成卡片堆：一排书脊横向滑动，点一下书前倾、书签升起；再点一下，书签抽出，成为那一页。

RECOMMENDATION

B — Lean and slip. It fixes the actual complaint: the facts rise out of the book you are pointing at, so the eye never leaves the spine and the white side card disappears. The tip shows the page block — the first moment these spines read as books — the slip still carries the real cover so nothing is lost from today's card, the arrow keys walk it, and it survives the phone as a tap-to-tip strip (M).

Combine: keep B's slip-becomes-page arrival for everyday clicks (≈0.6 s); borrow A's leaning neighbours for B's hover — cheap, and it gives the row weight; keep the bird on every shelf, on the plank lip in B (the slip owns the spine tops) and on the tops in A/C. A's full pull–turn–open is a ≈1.4 s ceremony: lovely once, tiring daily. C keeps the side glance and its sprite is too soft at this size. Series badges become paper labels and つづく goes pencil, so the pen is the only coral on the shelf.
