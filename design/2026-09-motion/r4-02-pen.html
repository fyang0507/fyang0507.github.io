THE POINTER

A separate mark, drawn by rule 指向：另一个记号，按规则画

the answer to “could be drawn arbitrarily bad”

Specimen sheet 标本页

each tile is a view with its own edge; the arrow is solved live from the tile’s real text. On the site only one of these exists per view.

Try to break it 试着把它画坏

Drag the target anywhere in the frame — or focus it and use the arrow keys (Shift = big steps). The arrow re-solves as it moves and redraws when you let go.

The audit drops the target at 400 random free spots, solves each, then re-measures every drawn arrow with the browser’s own path geometry: crossings, edges, length, bow, barbs.

RULE 1

One stroke, two tiers 一笔，两级

An element shows its highest tier. The lower tier is absorbed, not removed: the tier-1 line survives as the band’s darker bottom edge. At rest (tier 0) nothing carries a mark except prose links, which keep a 1px pencil hairline so they can be found. The line hangs from the baseline — baseline + max(3 px, 0.18 em) — and whatever follows it (the next line of text, or a drawn rule) stays ≥ 10 px below the stroke and ≥ 2× that drop, so the line belongs to the word above it; a crowded layout moves, the stroke never shrinks. The band is a shape — a bar behind the word — and aria-pressed / aria-current / aria-expanded say the same thing to a screen reader, so “chosen” never rests on colour alone. The pointer is not in this table: it is not a tier (Rule 3).

RULE 2

Up and down the tiers 升与降

0 → 1
pointer arrives: the coral underline draws left → right.
1 → 2
click / Enter: the pen doesn’t lift. It turns the chisel and comes back right → left; behind the tip the line thickens upward into the wheat band and its coral cools into the band’s edge as the band passes over it; the stroke ends on the chisel’s cut at the left.
0 → 2
tap or keyboard: the whole gesture at once — the coral line in 170 ms, then the return stroke absorbs it.
2 → 1
closed while still under the pointer: the band sinks back into its line, and the line warms back to coral as it does.
2 → 0
the choice moves elsewhere: band sinks → wheat line → line lifts, while the new choice draws. No coral: the pointer isn’t there.
press
momentary targets (links, Load more, ← →): 1 → 2 on press, held at least until the gesture lands, then back to 1.
interrupt
every reversal starts from the current frame. A half-grown band sinks from half height; nothing snaps.
RULE 3

The pointer is a separate mark 指向是另一个记号

what
a short coral arrow at the one thing a view asks of you. Tiers answer what you did; the pointer speaks first — and it has to travel, which is where arrows go wrong.
glyph
shaft 26–40 px whatever the distance · a hand bow ≤ 1.8 px in its first third, straight for the last, so it never S-bends or hooks and the head sits square on the line · two barbs ≈ 7 px at 25–30°, drawn barb → tip → barb with the same 2.2 px pen · the tip stops 4.5–6 px short.
from
eight fixed approaches in reading order: left, upper left, above, upper right, right, lower left, lower right, below. The first whose shaft, head and note clear every line of text, icon, pin and other card inside the view wins. Too tight → the same approach at 26 px. Nothing → drop the note. Still nothing → no arrow. A bad arrow is never drawn.
note
only when the target’s label doesn’t say why (“start here · 从这里开始” on a card; none on “Load more”). Tight at the tail like a margin note: stacked beside a sideways arrow, a row above a downward one. Hand fonts, soft ink — the note is the only part of the pointer that isn’t coral.
seed
length, bow side, barbs and the few degrees of lean are seeded by the target: the same arrow every time.
motion
note written 420 ms → the pen lifts back to the tail (60 ms) → shaft in one pass ≈ 240 ms → head 130 ms → one settle, 2 px toward the target for a held frame, resting at 1 px. Round 2’s two taps are gone: on a 30 px arrow they read as fidgeting.
budget
one per view, drawn when the view is first seen — the only coral that speaks before you do. Acting on the target lifts it (tail chases head) for the rest of the browser session (sessionStorage). Never on navigation.
RULE 4

Coral, touch, stillness 珊瑚色、触摸、静止

coral
Coral is the pen’s attention. It marks where the hand is right now — notice (hover) and focus (keyboard), both transient: they follow you and leave with you — plus the one point arrow per view, which retires once used, and the page title’s fixed character. At rest only the point and the title are coral. Chosen states — current page, selected tag, open card — are wheat, so coral is never spent on something that is merely true. Hovering near the arrow’s target briefly shows two corals; both are the pen, and it passes.
ink
one coral, #D9695A: 3.19:1 on cream. On tinted paper it is drawn as that paper shows ink — multiplied: #CB5E49 on cork (3.17:1), #C85E47 on the lead card’s wheat (3.20:1), where flat coral would be 2.72:1 and 2.69:1. The 「 」 around a card sit on the cork and take the cork value. One 2.2 px pen for all three marks.
title
the subpage title’s coral character (造 / 写) stays: fixed identity, not interaction.
so
resting link underlines and footnote numbers go pencil; the Building lead pin goes ink; round 3’s wheat-ink notice line (2.84:1 on cream) is gone.
touch
no tier 1 — nothing sticks after a finger lifts. A tap is tier 2 directly: the coral line in 170 ms and the return stroke absorbs it at once; momentary targets play it through, then sink to nothing.
keyboard
Tab shows coral 「 」 only. Enter / Space is the 0 → 2 gesture.
reduced
every mark appears in its end state, instantly: the band at once, the arrow complete and settled, no note-writing; retracts are instant.

RECOMMENDATION

A · one stroke, two tiers, a pointer — and the pen’s attention in coral. Coral now means “the hand is here”: the hover line, the focus brackets and the arrow share one ink and one 2.2 px pen. Choosing turns that attention into a decision, so the return stroke absorbs the coral into the wheat band, and a page at rest shows only its one arrow and its title character in coral. The underline hangs from the baseline with measured air below it, which is what fixes the crowded card titles.

Watch: coral on tinted paper must be the multiplied value, not flat #D9695A (which drops under 3:1 on cork and wheat) — in production that’s one --pen custom property per paper. The spacing rule relies on a baseline probe and real text boxes, and must be measured with card tilt removed. Two corals can meet for a moment when you hover near the arrow’s target; both are the pen, and it passes.
