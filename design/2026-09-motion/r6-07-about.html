BOARD 07 · ROUND 6

Only the sleeve takes it back 只有卡套能把它收回

Round 5 split flip from put-back by kind: a quick flick flips, a drop on the sleeve puts back, and anything else settles. That third outcome was the trouble. A drag that wasn't quick enough did nothing, so flipping felt tricky unless you clicked, and you couldn't tell which outcome you'd get until you let go.

evidence: r5-07-about-rig.js holdEnd() three outcomes, with flick thresholds (600 px/s, 170 px, 1400 px/s) and a catch exception (900 px/s, 100 ms). A slow drag in open space returned settle, so nothing happened.

FRED, ON ROUND 5

“I still think the flip is tricky to trigger (unless clicking on the card) which is unintuitive. Can we discern the ‘put back’ action vs. ‘flip’ method even more? I would suggest only recognize the movement to drag the card to the sleeve as the only eligible action to ‘put back’.”

Where you let go decides, and nothing else does. If the card's centre is over the sleeve, it goes back in. Anywhere else it flips, toward the way you dragged (or to the right if you only moved it up or down), at any speed and over any distance. A tap flips too. The flick thresholds, the "settle" outcome and the "can't catch a flung card" exception are gone, and so are the tap-the-sleeve and printed 放回 put-backs. Keyboard users keep Esc and a 放回 / PUT BACK button that appears when it has focus.

You see the outcome while you drag. Off the sleeve the card turns with your drag: 10° as soon as it moves, up to 40° after a third of a card's width. Let go and the flip completes from there while the card springs back to its place. Over the sleeve the turn relaxes and the sleeve takes the card half into its mouth, with the lip bowed and a coral 「 」. Drag back out and the turn returns. On a phone, lifting the card now also sets the sleeve aside to the left edge, level with the card, mouth toward it. So putting it back is a sideways drag there too, which a touch browser leaves to the page's own code (pan-y), and a vertical swipe still scrolls. The cost is 24 px of card width against r5 (316 instead of 340 at 390): the strip takes 36 px, and dropping the phone stage's side padding wins 12 back.

RECOMMENDATION

Ship this as the About card. The hand now has one rule that fits in a sentence: the sleeve takes it back; anywhere else it turns over. You can't reach an outcome you didn't see coming. The card turns while you drag it, and the sleeve visibly takes it before you let go. So a drag toward the sleeve that stops short still flips, and a flick near the sleeve flips too, because only the card's position counts, never its speed.

Watch: a long drag toward the sleeve does end up putting the card back. That's intended, since it is the one move Fred reserved for putting back, and the half-inserted card shows it before you let go. On a phone, a vertical swipe that starts on the card scrolls the page and doesn't flip; flipping there is a tap or a sideways drag. Setting the sleeve aside on a phone is one extra movement on lift. It has a cause (the other hand makes room and keeps the mouth ready), and the sleeve goes back to its desk spot once the card is in.
