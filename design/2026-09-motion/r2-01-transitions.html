The desk becomes the nav 桌子变成导航

Round 1’s A carried only the object you clicked into its tab. The site holds a bigger rhyme it never plays: the desk and the subpage header are the same drawing at two sizes, four objects standing on one line, and the table’s front edge is the nav’s 1.5px rule waiting to happen. Every navigation between them is still a hard cut.

evidence: index.html:131 the desk is one raster (laptop and camera baked in) · site-nav.css:31 the rule is a masked ::after · site-nav.css:43–53 90px slots, sprite 22px above the rule, ×.72 on phones · no @view-transition anywhere

click any object on the desk ↗
Click an object and the whole desk files itself into the header. The one you touched lifts first; the others follow in a ripple, each on an arc with its own mass (the laptop hauls, the frame flits). The table folds up into its front edge; the edge unhooks, straightens and rises, sagging in the middle like a line pulled by both ends, and lands as the 1.5px rule just as the objects set down on it. The mug, the plant and the bird have no slot: a held beat, then they drop. The pen draws the folder tab around your object and the page lands under it. Home reverses on gravity: the rule drops, the table springs open from it, the objects fall onto it and bounce (the laptop thuds), the props drop back in, the pen redraws the notes. Tab→tab: the folder tab slides on a spring while a hop is passed along the row, from the object you leave to the one you open, on held frames.

RECOMMENDATION

A — The desk becomes the nav. It is round 1’s A made into one event rather than a new idea: the desk turns into the header it was always drawn to be, so the site explains its own navigation. The surprise is physical and made of Fred’s own objects: the table folding into its edge, the edge becoming the rule, the mug and the plant dropping because their table left. No glow, no particles, no generic effect.

B is the more cinematic sibling, and the laptop lifted off its own screen is a real surprise, but it spends a second big motion in the same second and covers A’s best moment; it stays on the board for comparison. Watch in A: the rule must arrive just before the objects set down (they land on the shelf, not in the air); the props must be gone in under 0.4 s so they never compete with the rising line. It runs on every home↔page navigation, so it has to stay under a second and interruptible, and it does. Round 1’s episode card, if kept, should stay rationed to the first visit.
