BOARD 02 · ROUND 3

One stroke, two tiers — and a pointer 一笔两级，另有指向

Round 2 called the pen “three tiers”. Two of them really are one stroke: the highlighter’s thin edge notices, and the same pen coming back broad-side down chooses. The arrow was never that stroke said louder. A stroke sits under its word; an arrow has to travel, and in round 2 the layout decided how far and how bent — so the “start here” arrow came out long and curved. Round 3 keeps the stroke, drops its tick, and turns the arrow into a glyph placed by rules that would rather not draw than draw badly.

THE POINTER

A separate mark, drawn by rule 指向：另一个记号，按规则画

the answer to “could be drawn arbitrarily bad”

Specimen sheet 标本页

each tile is a view with its own edge; the arrow is solved live from the tile’s real text. On the site only one of these exists per view.

Try to break it 试着把它画坏

Drag the target anywhere in the frame — or focus it and use the arrow keys (Shift = big steps). The arrow re-solves as it moves and redraws when you let go.

The audit drops the target at 400 random free spots, solves each, then re-measures every drawn arrow with the browser’s own path geometry: crossings, edges, length, bow, barbs.

RULE 1

One stroke, two tiers 一笔，两级

An element shows its highest tier. The lower tier is absorbed, not removed: the tier-1 line survives as the band’s darker bottom edge. At rest (tier 0) nothing carries a mark except prose links, which keep a 1px pencil hairline so they can be found. The band is a shape — a bar behind the word — and aria-pressed / aria-current / aria-expanded say the same thing to a screen reader, so “chosen” never rests on colour alone. The pointer is not in this table: it is not a tier (Rule 3).

RULE 2

Up and down the tiers 升与降

0 → 1
pointer arrives: the underline draws left → right.
1 → 2
click / Enter: the pen doesn’t lift. It turns the chisel and comes back right → left; behind the tip the line thickens upward into the band, and the stroke ends on the chisel’s cut at the left.
0 → 2
tap or keyboard: the whole gesture at once — line in 170 ms, then the return stroke.
2 → 1
closed while still under the pointer: the band sinks back into its line.
2 → 0
the choice moves elsewhere: band sinks → line → line lifts, while the new choice draws.
press
momentary targets (links, Load more, ← →): 1 → 2 on press, held at least until the gesture lands, then back to 1.
interrupt
every reversal starts from the current frame. A half-grown band sinks from half height; nothing snaps.
RULE 3

The pointer is a separate mark 指向是另一个记号

what
a short coral arrow at the one thing a view asks of you. Tiers answer what you did; the pointer speaks first — and it has to travel, which is where arrows go wrong.
glyph
shaft 26–40 px whatever the distance · a hand bow ≤ 1.8 px in its first third, straight for the last, so it never S-bends or hooks and the head sits square on the line · two barbs ≈ 7 px at 25–30°, drawn barb → tip → barb with the same 2.2 px pen · the tip stops 4.5–6 px short.
from
eight fixed approaches in reading order: left, upper left, above, upper right, right, lower left, lower right, below. The first whose shaft, head and note clear every line of text, icon, pin and other card inside the view wins. Too tight → the same approach at 26 px. Nothing → drop the note. Still nothing → no arrow. A bad arrow is never drawn.
note
only when the target’s label doesn’t say why (“start here · 从这里开始” on a card; none on “Load more”). Tight at the tail like a margin note: stacked beside a sideways arrow, a row above a downward one. Hand fonts, soft ink — not coral.
seed
length, bow side, barbs and the few degrees of lean are seeded by the target: the same arrow every time.
motion
note written 420 ms → the pen lifts back to the tail (60 ms) → shaft in one pass ≈ 240 ms → head 130 ms → one settle, 2 px toward the target for a held frame, resting at 1 px. Round 2’s two taps are gone: on a 30 px arrow they read as fidgeting.
budget
coral is this mark’s alone — one per view, drawn when the view is first seen. Acting on the target lifts it (tail chases head) for the rest of the browser session (sessionStorage). Never on navigation.
RULE 4

Coral, touch, stillness 珊瑚色、触摸、静止

coral
The pointer owns it, alone. It is already rationed to one per view — the accent’s own budget. Tier 1 follows the pointer: coral there turns the accent into a cursor. Tier 2 is plural at rest — current page, selected tag, open card — so coral there spends it three times a view.
title
the subpage title’s coral character (造 / 写) stays: fixed identity, not interaction. A brief overlap with a first-view arrow is accepted.
so
resting link underlines and footnote numbers go pencil; the Building lead pin goes ink; a view whose arrow has been used has no interactive coral at all.
touch
no tier 1 — nothing sticks after a finger lifts. A tap is tier 2 directly (the 0 → 2 gesture); momentary targets play it through, then sink.
keyboard
Tab shows 「 」 only. Enter / Space is the 0 → 2 gesture.
reduced
every mark appears in its end state, instantly: the band at once, the arrow complete and settled, no note-writing; retracts are instant.

RECOMMENDATION

A · one stroke, two tiers, and a pointer that can’t be drawn badly. The stroke is round 2’s without the tick: notice is the highlighter’s thin edge, choose is the same pen coming back broad-side down, focus 「 」 stays apart. The pointer left the tiers because it is a different problem. It is now one glyph — 26–40 px, a bow under 2 px, 7 px barbs — placed by rules that try a fixed set of approaches and refuse to draw rather than cross text or run long.

Watch: the solver reads real text line boxes, so non-text objects that must stay clear (icons, pins, other cards) need an attribute in production (data-pen-ob; data-ob here). Solve after fonts load and again on resize. The floor case — no clean path, so no arrow — is shown on the sheet; the real targets on this board never hit it at 1440 or 390.
