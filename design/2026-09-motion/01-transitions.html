BOARD 01

Page transitions 转场

Every navigation is a hard cut followed by the same generic “fade and rise 14px” on every page. Yet the site sets up continuity perfectly and never delivers it: the laptop, book, frame and camera on the desk are the doors, and every subpage repeats the same four objects as its tab nav, the current one in a folder-tab outline with a coral dot. The object you click simply vanishes instead of arriving, and tab-to-tab hops between subpages hard-cut too.

evidence: index.html:184–195 desk hotspots → plain links · site-nav.css:47 active tab is a static border · Building.dc.html:189 Writing.dc.html:42 Gallery.dc.html:40 About.dc.html:34 the same 14px rise, four times · no @view-transition anywhere

AObject becomes tab 物归其位shared element · arc on a spring · pen-drawn tab · physics clock
click any object on the desk ↗
The object you click is picked up, carried on an arc with mass (one small overshoot, then it settles) and set down in its own slot in the arriving page’s tab nav; the rest of the desk recedes beneath it, the pen draws the folder tab around it, and the title lands last. “home” carries it back to the desk. Tab→tab: the outline and its coral dot slide to the new tab while the content swaps.
support cross-document View Transitions — @view-transition{navigation:auto} plus one view-transition-name per desk object and the matching .site-nav-object — morph object→tab with CSS alone in Chrome/Edge 126+ and Safari 18.2+; Firefox falls back to today’s hard cut. The arc and overshoot are ~40 lines of JS in pagereveal that re-key ::view-transition-group(obj) with these sampled keyframes (this demo previews the curve with WAAPI). · tab→tab pure CSS: view-transition-name:active-tab on the current tab’s outline + a spring written as linear() — this demo runs it as a real same-document startViewTransition. · a11y reduced motion = instant swap; the destination strip is readable by ~0.45 s, the object lands at ~0.5 s, and input is never blocked past the landing. · cost the pen outline replaces the CSS border on every page.
BDolly into the object 推镜入物one camera zoom about a fixed point · clip-path canvas · physics clock
click any object on the desk ↗
One committed push. The laptop’s blank screen, the book’s open spread, the frame’s picture or the camera’s lens is already showing the destination; the camera pushes in about a single fixed point until that surface fills the view, then it opens to the full page (the lens literally opens its aperture). Back pulls out along the same line; tab→tab trucks the camera sideways in tab order.
support not a shared element — desk and page have nothing in common to name — so cross-document VT only helps as a stage: ::view-transition-old(root) zoom keyframes and a new(root) clip-path, both computed per object in JS on pageswap/pagereveal (Chrome/Edge 126+, Safari 18.2+; Firefox hard cut). · cost the desk raster is upscaled ×4–10 mid-push and goes soft for ~150 ms; the largest motion on the board, on every click. · a11y a full-viewport zoom is exactly what prefers-reduced-motion exists for — instant swap there. · tab→tab the truck is the weak part: honest, but close to a generic slide.
CNichijou cut 日常式转场iris · held beat · stepped bird · hand’s clock
click any object on the desk ↗
The opener’s iris closes onto the thing you clicked and holds it for a beat, snaps shut, cuts to an episode card while one bird hops across on held frames, then opens on the same object where it now lives — its nav tab. It’s the site’s own joke, and it lands. But it costs ≈1.1 s per navigation: charming the first time, a tax by the fifth.
cost ~1.1 s of blocked view vs. ~0 for today’s cut; tab→tab and back drop the card and still cost ~0.7 s. · support cross-document: circle clip-path keyframes on ::view-transition-old(root) / new(root), card + bird as a transient element of the new page — but the close can only start once the next page is ready, so on a slow network the click feels dead. The JS alternative closes on click, navigates under the black (the beat becomes an honest loading screen) and opens on pagereveal. · a11y reduced motion = instant swap; the black is the opener’s warm #17120C, never a white flash.
RECOMMENDATION

A — Object becomes tab. It is the only candidate that pays off what the site already built: the desk object and the nav object are the same drawing, and A literally carries one into the other, so the nav finally explains itself. It is also the cheapest to ship honestly — the morph and the tab slide are CSS-only cross-document View Transitions; only the arc and overshoot need a little JS, and Firefox simply keeps today’s cut.

Combine: keep C’s episode card, but ration it like the existing opener — the first home→subpage navigation of a session only, never on back or tab→tab. Leave B on the board as the cinematic reference; it spends the whole motion budget on the camera instead of the object. Watch in A: the desk drawing and the nav sprite differ (the nav laptop carries the ghost sticker, the nav book is steeper), and the crossfade over the middle of the arc (30–75%) is what hides that — don’t lengthen it. Keep landing under ~0.5 s and never make the title wait on the pen. Give the identity lockup its own view-transition-name so it never blinks between pages.
