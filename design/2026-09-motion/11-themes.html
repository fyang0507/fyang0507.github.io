BOARD 11 · ANSWER TO “PAPER LOCKS US INTO ONE THEME”

One drawing, any light 一张画，任何光线

You’re right that the lock-in is real. But it lives in the assets and the plumbing, not in the paper idea. Each illustration is a PNG with the pen colour and opaque cream fills baked in, so it only looks right on cream. Every page also re-declares the palette as hex. Separate the drawing from its colour once, and a theme becomes a token change instead of an asset project.

evidence: 237 literal colours across 9 files · 8 separate :root palettes · 24 per-theme illustration files (15 -light, 9 -dark) · masters are 15–50% opaque cream fill (desk 15%, portrait 48%, book 50%)

01Today 现状

These are the exact PNGs the site ships, on paper and then on a night field. The line art itself would survive the dark. The baked cream fills turn every object into a glowing cut-out, which is why the nav and bird were hand-exported a second time as -dark files and why the gateway pages were declared light-only.

02Role layers, live 角色分层

The same masters, split by a generator into three layers: ink (how much of each pixel is pen), fill (how much is paper hiding what’s behind) and tint (the few drawn colours: the bird’s yellow, the cat on the mug, the ghost on the camera). Tokens colour ink and fill, and tint stays as drawn. The sprites animate exactly as before. Switch the theme, then press parity and drag across the desk to compare against the shipped PNG.

04Where polarity can’t flip 不能反相的地方

Your catch. Line-only art reads the same inverted: the nav frame’s cat, the laptop, the plant. Continuous grey tone doesn’t. The portrait’s halftone hair and shading only mean something dark-on-light. In negative the pupils go white and the hair goes grey, and the face stops being that face, which is why photographic negatives of people are hard to read. The generator now finds the print inside the frame from pixels alone: it takes the paper region around a seed and closes it to a convex sheet, in all four expressions. One line of config per master says “this holds a toned print”.

B fixed the face but introduced a second medium. The scene is chalk on a dark board, and B pastes a lit paper sheet into it. So the print has to be drawn in the same chalk as everything else, and the face has to survive that. (I also tried a chalk halftone of the positive. At real sizes the dot screen is coarser than the face and reads as a checkerboard, so I dropped it.)

D keeps the print’s strong strokes as chalk. The solid dark masses (the pupils) are found by shape, as a matched pair wider than any stroke. They stay dark and get a thin chalk rim, so the eyes still look at you instead of glowing. The grey tone becomes its own layer with one strength setting. The ladder below shows that one setting at four fixed values, which is where the choice actually is: from a clean chalk drawing (the canon’s “people get airier”) to nearly the old negative. The book is an ordinary world object: dark pages, with the faint text boosted into readable chalk dashes.

At the size the portrait actually lives on the desk, tone is nearly invisible: 0% and 100% differ by about 3/255. So the knob is really a close-up decision, for the About page or a zoom. My call is 0% on the desk (the cleanest chalk drawing) and about 35% wherever the portrait is shown large.

Tone on darkimplicit: everything is dark-on-creamOne medium per scene. On a dark field everything is drawn in the world’s one chalk. Faint line-like tone (a book’s text) gets a gain, and a toned print (the portrait) is reduced to chalk contour: its tone is dropped, and its pupils are found as a matched pair of dark masses, kept dark and rimmed in chalk. It’s declared per master and written to roles.json, and the renderer picks the treatment from the field. Photos and covers are content, not drawings: they keep their colour and get a mat.
Stays raster—Photos, essay covers and the About stand figure are content, not chrome. In dark themes they keep their colour and get a paper mat (field-raised border) plus a 4% dim, never a filter that changes the image.
RECOMMENDATION

Do the decoupling now, whether or not dark mode ships. It’s one generator step and one tokens file, and it removes the reason every breaking change feels expensive: nothing visual stays welded to a colour. Then dark mode (or seasonal paper, or a print theme) becomes a cheap, reversible decision you can make on taste alone.

Sequence it as an “L0.5” between hygiene and the motion system, because boards 01 and 09 move these very sprites around and should move the role-layered versions. Night gets its own small art-direction pass later: which accents survive a dark field, and how strong the grain is.
