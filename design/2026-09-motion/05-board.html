BOARD 05

The corkboard, with weight 软木板

The corkboard is the right metaphor, but it behaves like a carousel: an overflow strip with ←/→ buttons, cards that ignore being moved, a heavy drop shadow under the board, and a hover that slides each card — and its pin — 4px up the cork. The lead card's “Fred Agent” wordmark runs an orbiting three-hue gradient on a 7-second loop: gradient plus motion with no cause, the most off-taste thing on the site. Pinned paper should hang: trail the board, settle when it stops, lift off its pin when touched, and otherwise hold perfectly still.

  1. Swipe sideways: the board follows the thumb 1:1, then coasts on the release velocity. The slips trail your swipe, swing past once when the board stops, and go still.
  2. Swipe up or down: the page scrolls as it always has — the board only claims horizontal gestures (touch-action: pan-y). No scroll-jacking.
  3. Past the last card: the cork gives a little (rubber-band resistance) and springs back; the paper takes that bump too.
  4. Tap: links and “+” work as today; a touch that ended a drag never follows a link.
  5. Reduced motion: the board still pans under the thumb, but never coasts or springs, and the paper never swings.

RECOMMENDATION

Pick A. It answers the actual complaint: the board stops being a carousel and becomes paper on cork — it has weight when you move it, every movement has a cause (your hand, the board's edge, the tape), and at rest it is completely still. Every card, link and layout stays exactly as Fred has it; no new UI is added, and the heavy board shadow goes.

Combine it with the flat-ink wordmark and coral pen underline (then the lead card's label dot and meta slashes go ink). Keep C for later as the replacement for the “+” popover, which today blurs in from 11px; if C lands, its flight should start from A's live swing angle. Skip B: the thread spends the accent on decoration and turns a portfolio into an evidence wall. Watch the tuning: the swing is soft-limited to ~8.6° and damped to one overshoot — any looser and it reads as rubber, not paper.
