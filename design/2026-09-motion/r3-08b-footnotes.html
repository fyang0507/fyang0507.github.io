WHAT CHANGED
FROM ROUND 2
Agree with C, but the sidenote sticker design should be A.
  • C’s gesture stays whole. A corner ⌜ where the claim starts, then one stroke without lifting: ⌟ where it ends, a loop round the number, and an arrow flung through the leading into the margin. It is one careful stroke and one fast one, with the full choreography once and then a quick repeat.
  • C’s catalogue card is gone. The note is A’s slip, with its paper, hand-cut edges, seeded tilt, punched eyelet and typography, and it is the same object at rest and in use.
  • The arrow replaces A’s thread. The arrow is drawn level along the ref’s leading, and its point stops at the rim of the spot where the eyelet will rest. As the point lands, the slip is jerked there by its eyelet. It lands 26px clear of the text, not A’s 14px, so the arrowhead has room in the gutter. It pivots on the eyelet, its trailing edge lags the pull, and it squares up to the text on A’s spring with one small overshoot. There is no thread and no knot. Because the slip comes to the arrow, the arrow stays level and straight. It no longer bends down to reach a note that sits lower than its ref.
  • Coral budget: the pen is the only coral in the reading view. The thread is gone, the underline C drew on the card’s title is gone, and body links, hover and focus go to ink. Citations stay upright serif with utility-mono dates, never handwriting.
  • Phone: it is still the slip pulled out of a slot cut in the page, and you swipe it back in. It is restyled as A’s paper, with the eyelet before the number and the citation set as A sets it, with live links.

RECOMMENDATION

Ship this one build. It keeps what made C the pick: the WOW carries information. The ⌜⌟ shows which words the source backs, the loop shows which number, and the arrow shows where the source is. It keeps what Fred liked in A: the note is a real piece of paper with an eyelet, and it answers the pen physically. Where round 2 needed a second coral line to pull the slip, the arrow now does it, so one gesture carries both the pointing and the pull. The first reveal takes under a second, and every one after is a stroke and a swing of about half a second.

Watch three things. A slip pulled up to its ref can overlap the slip above it for as long as it is held (the held one is always on top); on a page dense with notes, consider nudging the neighbour. The phrase finder is a heuristic, so author-marked spans would make the bracket exact. And the site nav’s current-tab dot is coral; it sits above the essay and scrolls away before any note, but board 02’s pen states should settle it for every page.
