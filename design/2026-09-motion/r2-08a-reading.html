WHAT CHANGED FROM ROUND 1
ANot all essays have the segments in 1.0, 2.0 etc., plus the current version has a bug when scrolled till the end.

Landmarks now come from whatever the essay has. First come numbered markers in any form (1.0, （一）, I., (3), 序 / Preface / 后记). Then headings: real h2/h3 and whole-line bold titles. Then figures and pull quotes. An essay with none gets minute ticks at paragraph starts (“3 min”), and a long unmarked stretch inside a structured essay is filled the same way. End of scroll: the rail is sticky inside the essay’s own column, so it leaves with the essay instead of lying over the ending. Over the last screen the reading line sweeps to the bottom, so at 100% the last landmark is reached and the line closes with a ✓, even on a 3-minute essay. Bounce and overscroll read as the nearest end.

DNot clearly showcased because the transition/dissolve effect is not obvious. There’s also the interplay with padding/margin to the body content.

The cover is now a plate pinned behind the top of the window, and reading prints paper over it. Just ahead of the paper, the cover is re-screened as a coarse colour halftone. Its dots stay registered to the plate and shrink in place, band by band, until only paper is left. The paper edge is tied to the text and always sits 84px above the first line, so there is never an empty screen and text never sits on the cover. The title rides slower than the page and flies into the nav; a wrapped title condenses into one line. When the paper reaches the top, the nav settles from 76 to 56px and a pencil hairline is drawn outward from the title. The same pen then goes on down the margin to draw A’s rail.

Start at the top and scroll slowly. The cover’s lower edge is already a halftone. As you read, the paper edge climbs with the text and the dots shrink row by row, while the title shrinks and travels into the nav. The moment the paper reaches the top, the nav settles, the hairline is drawn out from the title, and the pen carries on down the left margin to draw the rail: ticks, labels, and the coral loop on the landmark you are in. Hover or focus a label to preview it; click to go there. Switch essays to see the rail follow each essay’s own structure. On a phone the hairline is the rail, with ticks and a “3.0 / 10” counter.

THE LAYOUT PLAN · 版式数字

Hero height, the body’s top padding and the nav’s height change are one plan, not three settings. The intro’s bottom padding is gap + Z + pad, so the body starts exactly pad below solid paper. The paper edge moves with the text, so that distance holds at every scroll position. It reaches the top of the window at sL = B0 − pad. At that moment the first line sits at pad = n1 + 28, just as the nav settles to n1.

RECOMMENDATION

Ship A and D together as the Reading page. D’s print-over is now the one dense event on the page: the cover turns into its own halftone and gives way to paper as you begin, the title condenses into the nav, and a single pen stroke hands over to A’s margin. After that, the page stays quiet, with a graphite line and one coral loop. The overlap bug is fixed structurally. The nav is opaque at every position and paints its own slice of the plate, so text cannot show through it.

Watch three things in production. First, the halftone samples the cover through a canvas, so covers must stay same-origin (they are, in images/derived/). Second, dark photographic covers print heavier dots; the dot ink is held back on dark cells, but check new covers at the top of the page. Third, the rail needs about 180px of left margin, so below roughly 1000px wide it becomes the phone strip. Footnotes (board B) float in the right margin and do not compete with the rail.
